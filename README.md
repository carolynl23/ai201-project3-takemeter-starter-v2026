## What This Does

<!-- Your community, and what your classifier sorts posts into. Three or four
     sentences. -->

My community is r/nba, Reddit's main NBA basketball forum. Its posts and
comments mix game-thread reactions, player and team debates, and stat-heavy
breakdowns. TakeMeter sorts each post by what kind of take it is: an argument
built on a checkable fact like a stat line, a record or a contract number; a
confident claim with nothing behind it; or a gut response to a game or play
that just happened. The goal is to separate posts that actually make a case
from the noise that fills every thread after a big game. Alot of the comments have numbers to back up their hot takes. Comparing players is a common trend.

## Label Taxonomy

<!-- Each label: a one-sentence definition and two real examples from your
     reading. Then your decision rule for the hardest boundary.

     The decision rule is worth a point on its own and it's the thing most
     people leave out. Every taxonomy has a hardest boundary. Name yours. -->

### `analysis`

**Definition:** Makes an argument about basketball backed by a specific, checkable fact (a stat, a contract or salary figure, a named game or play, a quote, a date) where the fact is the reason for the conclusion.

**Example 1:**
> NBA players collectively make 51% of the league's basketball related income. If you drop that number down I fucking promise you the owners wouldn't just hand the surplus to doctors and teachers.

**Example 2:**
> I mean, I think there was definitely some confidence issues for him, he passed up a dunk on Trae Young to win a playoff game. And when he came back he wouldn't even attempt a 3.

### `hot_take`

**Definition:** A confident general claim about a player, team or the league (rankings, "best ever", predictions, "he's washed") with no checkable support offered; strong feeling is not support.

**Example 1:**
> He's the best basketball player I've ever seen. I don't give a fuck, you all know it's true. The accomplishments, the awards, etc etc will all come but this is the most talented basketball player ever.

**Example 2:**
> I think he fell out of love with basketball. I don't know how more guys don't. It's like from 12 until forever it's all basketball all the time.

### `reaction`

**Definition:** An immediate emotional response to a play, game or piece of news that just happened, about what the writer saw or felt rather than a claim about the world.

**Example 1:**
> WHAT THE FUCK MAN. Honestly the saddest NBA news speaking as a person instead of just a fan. One day I read how happy he is and how much he needed the time off to get right physically and mentally. Earns a starting spot. And now this? Insane.

**Example 2:**
> He crossed the court in 4 dribbles, 4 dribbles!

### The hardest boundary

**Which two labels:** `analysis` vs `hot_take`. On r/nba, lots of hot takes come with a number attached.

**Borderline post:**
> he started his euro step a foot behind the 3 point line... i think that's a first in NBA history.

**The decision rule I used every time:**
> e.g. "If the post names a specific checkable fact, it's `analysis`, even if the tone is heated."

## The Dataset

<!-- Where you collected from, how you labelled, your counts, and three hard
     cases. -->

**Where the posts came from:** Public comments from six r/nba threads in October 2026: the Ben Simmons Achilles injury news, Wemby's euro-step self-lob highlight, Blake Griffin's comments on athletes' egos, the Steve Kerr look-alike clip, an Onion article about LeBron, and the Cavs practicing in Cleveland's Public Hall. Claude wrote a script that pulled 470 comments from Reddit's public RSS feeds (`data/raw_nba_comments.csv`). From those I kept 215 and dropped pure jokes, bare links, and off-topic threads about whether Philadelphia or Cleveland is a nice city. Every post is kept whole.

**How I labelled them:** I labelled the first 20 rows cold, using only my definitions (`note` = `cold`). Claude pre-labelled the other 195 from my definitions, and I read every one (`note` = `pre-labelled, checked`). I agreed with 194 and changed one, from `hot_take` to `analysis` (the post about Yao being overworked before he reached the NBA).

**Counts per label:**

| Label | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| `hot_take` | 97 | 45.1% |
| `reaction` | 85 | 39.5% |
| `analysis` | 33 | 15.3% |
| **Total** | 215 | 100% |

**Known weakness: `analysis` is thin.** It's only about 15% of the file, below the 20% floor in my criterion 1. I'm keeping the data as collected rather than padding it. All six threads came from r/nba's hot and top pages that week, which were mostly highlights, memes and injury news. Those threads are full of reactions and confident opinions, and stat-backed arguments are rare. Discussion and post-game threads would likely have more `analysis`, but Reddit's rate limit meant I only reached six threads. Expect this to show up next unit: `analysis` will have only about 5–6 posts in the test split, so its F1 will swing between seeds, and the model may lean toward `hot_take` on borderline posts.
